What this project actually runs

The AI service predicts attrition risk — a supervised binary classification problem on tabular HR data, served by XGBoost through FastAPI, with the Claude API generating the natural-language explanation around the score. So Parts I–III are the working parts; Parts IV and V are context you need to read the field and to know what not to reach for.

Part I

Maths and statistics

Maths 01

What maths you actually need

AreaDepthWhy, concretely
StatisticsDeepThe whole job. Deciding whether a difference is real, whether a sample represents anything, whether a metric moved or wobbled.
ProbabilitySolidEvery classifier outputs a probability. Reading it correctly — especially against a low base rate — is where most misinterpretation happens.
Linear algebraConceptualData is a matrix; a row is a vector; similarity is a distance or an angle. You need the picture, not hand computation.
CalculusIntuition onlyA derivative is a slope; training walks downhill on an error surface. That sentence is enough — you will never differentiate anything by hand.
Optimisation theoryNot neededThe library does it. Knowing that it can get stuck in a poor solution is the useful part.
The three pictures worth carrying
①  DATA IS A MATRIX
     rows    = observations   (one employee)
     columns = features       (tenure, satisfaction, salary band)
   ⇒ so "the model takes X and predicts y" means: take this
     grid of numbers, produce one column.
   ⭐ AND EVERY ALGORITHM NEEDS NUMBERS. Text, categories and
     dates must be converted — that conversion is feature
     engineering (reflex 07), and it is where most of the real
     work lives.

②  A ROW IS A POINT IN SPACE
     two features = a point on a plane. Fifty features = a point
     in fifty dimensions, which you cannot picture but the maths
     handles identically.
   ⇒ SIMILARITY BECOMES DISTANCE. This is why clustering,
     nearest-neighbour methods and embeddings all work: "alike"
     means "close".
   ⚠ AND WHY SCALE MATTERS: salary in the tens of thousands and
     satisfaction between 0 and 1 are not comparable distances.
     Salary dominates every distance calculation purely because
     of its units — hence scaling (reflex 06).

③  TRAINING IS WALKING DOWNHILL
     the model has knobs (parameters) and an error measure.
     GRADIENT DESCENT: measure the slope of the error with
     respect to each knob, step in the downhill direction,
     repeat.
   ⭐ THE LEARNING RATE IS THE STEP SIZE. Too large and you
     bounce past the bottom and diverge; too small and it takes
     forever. That is the whole reason it is the first
     hyperparameter anyone tunes.
Statistics 02

Describing data

Centre, spread, shape — and the traps in each
CENTRE
  MEAN     the average. ⚠ dragged by extremes.
  MEDIAN   the middle value. ⭐ USE THIS FOR SALARY, TENURE,
           DURATION — anything skewed. A single executive salary
           moves a mean and not a median.
  MODE     the most common value. The only one that works for
           categories.

⭐ THE HABIT: NEVER REPORT A MEAN ALONE. Mean with median, or
  mean with a distribution. "Average tenure is 4.2 years" hides
  whether that is everyone at four years, or half at one year
  and half at eight — which are completely different companies.

SPREAD
  RANGE               max − min. Fragile: one outlier defines it.
  STANDARD DEVIATION  typical distance from the mean.
  IQR                 the middle 50%. ⭐ robust to outliers, and
                      the basis of the box plot and of the usual
                      outlier rule.
  ⚠ TWO GROUPS CAN SHARE A MEAN AND BE NOTHING ALIKE. Spread is
    not a secondary detail; it is often the finding.

SHAPE
  SKEW      a long tail one way. Income, tenure and durations are
            almost always right-skewed.
  MODALITY  two humps usually means TWO POPULATIONS mixed
            together. ⭐ splitting them is often the analysis.

PERCENTILES — the most useful and least used summary
  p50 median · p90 · p99
  ⇒ "the average request takes 200 ms" while p99 is 8 seconds
    means one user in a hundred has a terrible time, and the
    mean will never tell you.

⚠ AND THE SCALE RULE FROM VOLUME I, RESTATED: what arithmetic is
  permitted depends on what the numbers mean. Averaging a
  satisfaction score of 1–5 assumes the gap from 1 to 2 equals
  the gap from 4 to 5. It does not, and the resulting "3.7
  average satisfaction" is a number with no defined meaning.
Statistics 03

Inference — is this real?

THE QUESTION EVERY STAKEHOLDER ASKS "attrition went from 12% to 15% — is that a problem?" AND THE THREE ANSWERS, IN ORDER ① IS IT SIGNAL OR NOISE? (common vs special cause) every process varies. A number that moves every month within a stable band has told you nothing when it moves again. ⭐ YOU CANNOT ANSWER "IS THIS A CHANGE?" WITHOUT KNOWING THE NORMAL RANGE OF VARIATION. ⇒ so the first thing to plot is the last 24 months, not the last 2. ② HOW BIG IS THE SAMPLE? 12% → 15% out of 800 people is worth investigating. Out of 20 people it is two extra leavers, which is Tuesday. ⚠ AND THE SUBGROUP TRAP: slice 800 employees by department, tenure and level and you have cells of nine. Every dramatic percentage in a small cell is noise, and slicing until something looks dramatic is how people mislead themselves sincerely. ③ IS IT MATERIAL? ⭐ THREE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS PEOPLE COLLAPSE INTO ONE: STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT — unlikely to be chance. With enough data, almost everything is. MATERIAL — big enough to act on. STABLE — still there next month. ⇒ a significant, immaterial, unstable difference is a common and expensive thing to reorganise a team around. WHAT A p-VALUE IS AND IS NOT IS "if there were no real effect, how surprising would this data be?" IS NOT the probability that the effect is real IS NOT a measure of how big the effect is ⚠ p < 0.05 IS A CONVENTION, NOT A LAW OF NATURE. And testing twenty things guarantees roughly one false positive at that threshold — which is why deciding what you will test BEFORE looking is a discipline and not pedantry. ⭐ THE BASE-RATE TRAP — the single most useful thing in this section, and directly relevant to this project a model flags attrition risk. It catches 90% of leavers and wrongly flags 10% of stayers. It flags an employee. What is the chance they leave? take 1,000 employees, 5% of whom actually leave: actual leavers 50 → 45 correctly flagged actual stayers 950 → 95 wrongly flagged total flagged 140 ⇒ of 140 flagged, 45 leave. THAT IS 32%. the model is good, and TWO THIRDS OF THE PEOPLE IT FLAGS WILL STAY. Nothing is wrong with the model — the rare event makes it so. ⇒ this is why a flag must be framed as "worth a conversation", never as "will leave", and why acting automatically on one would be indefensible.
Statistics 04

Correlation and effect size

What a correlation does and does not license
r RUNS FROM −1 TO +1 and measures LINEAR association only.
  ⚠ a perfect curve can have r near zero. ⭐ ALWAYS PLOT IT —
    identical correlation coefficients hide completely different
    shapes, including one caused entirely by a single outlier.

CORRELATION IS NOT CAUSATION, and the useful part is knowing WHY
NOT — there are four alternatives, always:

  ① REVERSE       low satisfaction → leaving, or is leaving
                  already decided and satisfaction the symptom?
  ② CONFOUNDING   a third thing drives both. ⭐ THE COMMON CASE.
                  Department drives both salary and attrition, so
                  salary "predicts" attrition without causing it.
  ③ SELECTION     your data only contains a filtered group.
                  Surveys of current employees cannot tell you
                  why people left — those people are gone.
  ④ CHANCE        with enough columns, some pair correlates.

⭐ THE HONEST PHRASINGS, WHICH ARE ALSO THE DEFENSIBLE ONES
  "X is associated with Y"
  "X predicts Y in this data"
  "employees with X are 2.3× more likely to Y"
  ⇒ all true and all careful. "X causes Y" requires an
    experiment or a very carefully argued design, and in HR data
    you almost never have one.

EFFECT SIZE — the number that is missing from most analysis
  significance says "probably not nothing".
  ⭐ EFFECT SIZE SAYS "AND IT IS THIS BIG", which is the only
    version a decision can use.
  ⇒ report the difference in the units people care about: three
    percentage points, eleven people, £40,000 of replacement
    cost. Not just "significant at p < 0.01".

REGRESSION TO THE MEAN — the trap that manufactures fake wins
  pick the worst-performing teams, intervene, measure again:
  they improve. So would they have without you, because extreme
  values are partly luck and luck does not persist.
  ⭐ THIS IS WHY A CONTROL GROUP MATTERS even when it feels
    unnecessary — and why "we targeted the worst and they got
    better" is not evidence of anything.
Part II

The toolkit

Toolkit 05

numpy and pandas

Two libraries, one idea: operate on whole columns
NUMPY — the array
  a typed, fixed-size, n-dimensional array with VECTORISED
  operations:
    arr * 2            every element, in one operation
    arr[arr > 0.5]     boolean masking — select by condition

  ⭐ WHY IT IS FAST: the loop happens in compiled code, not in
    Python. A Python for-loop over a million rows is roughly a
    hundred times slower than the same work vectorised.
    ⇒ SO THE RULE IS: NO LOOPS OVER ROWS. If you are writing
      `for i in range(len(df))`, there is a vectorised form and
      it is both faster and shorter.

PANDAS — the DataFrame
  a table with labelled columns and an index. numpy underneath,
  spreadsheet-shaped on top.

  THE OPERATIONS THAT COVER MOST WORK
    df[df.tenure > 5]                       filter rows
    df[['name','risk']]                     select columns
    df.groupby('department').agg({…})        ⭐ the workhorse
    df.merge(teams, on='team_id', how='left')  a join
    df.pivot_table(...)                     reshape
    df.sort_values('risk', ascending=False)

  ⚠ THE FOUR THINGS THAT WILL CATCH YOU
    · CHAINED ASSIGNMENT — df[df.x > 1]['y'] = 0 may modify a
      copy and silently do nothing. Use .loc[] for assignment.
    · THE INDEX IS NOT A COLUMN, and operations that realign on
      it produce surprising NaNs. reset_index() liberally.
    · NaN IS NOT EQUAL TO ITSELF, so == never finds it. Use
      .isna().
    · MEMORY: a DataFrame holds everything at once. A 2 GB CSV
      is not a 2 GB DataFrame — it is often several times larger.

⭐ THE GROUPBY MENTAL MODEL: SPLIT the rows into groups, APPLY a
  function to each, COMBINE the results. Once that clicks, most
  analytical questions become one line.
Toolkit 06

Cleaning

Missing, duplicate, wrong, and out of range
MISSING VALUES — first ask WHY, because it decides what is allowed
  MCAR  missing at random, unrelated to anything. Rare, and the
        only case where dropping rows is safe.
  MAR   missingness depends on something you HAVE (managers
        skipped a field). Can be modelled.
  MNAR  ⭐ missingness depends on the missing value ITSELF.
        The unhappiest employees skip the satisfaction survey.
        ⇒ ANY imputation here biases the result, and the fact
          that a value is missing is itself information — so
          keep it as a feature rather than filling it in.

  THE OPTIONS
    drop rows        only if few and MCAR
    drop the column  if mostly empty
    impute           median (robust) · mode (categories) ·
                     forward-fill (time series) · model-based
    ⭐ AND ALWAYS: ADD A `was_missing` FLAG COLUMN.
      An imputed value that looks measured is worse than a null,
      because nobody downstream can tell. The flag is often
      predictive in its own right.

DUPLICATES
  exact duplicates are easy. ⚠ near-duplicates are the real
  problem: the same person with two spellings, two ids, two
  email addresses. Decide which record survives — by recency,
  completeness, or source priority — and record the rule.

TYPES AND FORMATS
  ⚠ leading zeros destroyed by a numeric read; dates in three
    formats in one column; a decimal comma; a number stored as
    text with a trailing space. All of these look fine in a
    preview and break a join.

OUTLIERS — and the question that must come first
  ⭐ IS IT AN ERROR OR A REAL EXTREME?
    a salary of 1 is an error. A salary of 400,000 is a real
    executive. ⚠ DELETING THE SECOND DELETES THE FINDING.
  the options: keep · cap (winsorise) · transform (log) ·
  remove · keep-and-flag ← usually the honest default.

SCALING — required by some algorithms, irrelevant to others
  standardise (mean 0, sd 1) or normalise (0–1).
  ⭐ NEEDED BY: anything distance-based (KNN, clustering, SVM)
    and neural networks.
  ⭐ NOT NEEDED BY: trees and tree ensembles — including the
    XGBoost this project uses, which is why you will not see
    scaling in the attrition pipeline.

⚠ THE RULE THAT PREVENTS THE WORST BUG IN THIS PART:
  FIT EVERY TRANSFORMATION ON THE TRAINING SET ONLY, then apply
  it to the test set. Computing a median or a scaler over the
  whole dataset leaks test information into training — see
  reflex 13.
Toolkit 07

Feature engineering

Usually worth more than changing the algorithm
⭐ THE FIELD'S MOST RELIABLE FINDING: BETTER FEATURES BEAT A
  BETTER MODEL, almost always. Swapping random forest for
  XGBoost might buy a point. A feature that encodes something
  the model could not otherwise see can buy ten.

WHAT TO CREATE — with HR examples
  RATIOS AND RATES   salary ÷ department median  ⭐ far more
                     informative than salary alone, because it
                     encodes "underpaid relative to peers"
  DIFFERENCES        months since last promotion; time since
                     last manager change
  DATE PARTS         tenure in years; hired in a reorganisation
                     year; month of hire
  AGGREGATES         team average satisfaction, team attrition
                     rate — ⚠ computed EXCLUDING the row itself,
                     or you have leaked the target
  FLAGS              was_missing; is_first_job; recently_relocated
  BINS               tenure bands, where the effect is not
                     linear — but binning throws information
                     away, so only where it genuinely helps

CATEGORIES → NUMBERS
  ONE-HOT      one column per value. Safe default for low
               cardinality.
               ⚠ 400 job titles becomes 400 columns.
  ORDINAL      only where order is real (junior < senior).
               ⚠ encoding cities as 1..50 invents an order the
                 model will happily use.
  TARGET ENC.  replace a category with its mean outcome.
               ⭐ POWERFUL AND LEAKY — must be computed within
                 the training folds only.

FEATURE SELECTION — fewer is often better
  drop near-constant columns, drop one of any highly correlated
  pair, and drop anything you cannot explain.
  ⭐ AND IN THIS DOMAIN, DROP DELIBERATELY: age, gender,
    ethnicity, marital status, and their PROXIES — postcode,
    school, name-derived anything. A model that predicts
    attrition partly from a protected characteristic is a legal
    problem regardless of accuracy, and proxies mean removing
    the obvious column is not sufficient.

⭐ THE TEST FOR ANY NEW FEATURE: WOULD THIS VALUE HAVE BEEN
  KNOWN AT PREDICTION TIME? If not, it is leakage — reflex 13.
Toolkit 08

Exploratory data analysis

The checklist, in order of cost to run
①  SHAPE AND SANITY
   how many rows and columns? one row per WHAT? does the count
   match what the business believes it should be?
   ⭐ IF THE ROW COUNT SURPRISES YOU, STOP AND RESOLVE IT. Every
     conclusion after this point inherits the error.

②  PER-COLUMN PROFILE
   type · missing % · unique count · min/max · top values
   ⇒ this one pass finds most data problems: a date of
     1970-01-01 (someone cast a zero), a satisfaction of 999,
     a "N/A" string in a numeric column, a category with 4,000
     distinct values that should have 6.

③  DISTRIBUTIONS, ONE VARIABLE AT A TIME
   histogram for numbers, bar chart for categories.
   looking for: skew, two humps, impossible values, a spike at
   one value that means "default" rather than "measured".

④  RELATIONSHIPS, TWO AT A TIME
   numeric vs numeric → scatter
   numeric vs category → box plot by group
   category vs category → cross-tab with percentages
   ⚠ percentages OF WHAT? Row, column and total percentages tell
     three different stories from one table.

⑤  THE TARGET, SPECIFICALLY
   ⭐ WHAT IS THE BASE RATE? If 5% of employees leave, that
     number governs your metric choice, your threshold and
     every interpretation (reflex 03). Find it first.

⑥  TIME
   plot the target by month. Looking for: a step change (a
   system migration or policy change), a gap (a failed load),
   a cliff at the end (⭐ AN INCOMPLETE PERIOD — the most
   common false alarm in reporting), and seasonality.

⭐ AND THE HABIT THAT SEPARATES ANALYSIS FROM GUESSING:
  QUANTIFY EVERY FINDING TWICE — by row count and by value.
  "3% of rows are affected" and "those rows are 40% of total
  salary" are both true and lead to opposite decisions.
Part III

Machine learning

ML 09

What machine learning is

Learning the rule from examples instead of writing it
TRADITIONAL   you write the rules.       data + rules → answers
ML            you supply examples.       data + answers → RULES
                                          then: rules + new data
                                          → predictions

⭐ SO ML IS WORTH IT ONLY WHEN THE RULE IS TOO COMPLEX OR TOO
  UNSTABLE TO WRITE DOWN. "Flag anyone with satisfaction below
  0.3 and no promotion in three years" is a rule — it is
  auditable, instant, free, explainable, and often nearly as
  good. ⚠ IF A RULE WORKS, USE THE RULE.

THE THREE FAMILIES
  SUPERVISED     labelled examples. ⭐ WHAT THIS PROJECT DOES:
                 historical employees, known outcome (left /
                 stayed), predict for current employees.
                 · CLASSIFICATION → a category   ← attrition
                 · REGRESSION     → a number

  UNSUPERVISED   no labels. Find structure.
                 · clustering, dimensionality reduction,
                   anomaly detection

  REINFORCEMENT  learn by acting and receiving reward. Games,
                 robotics, control. ⚠ NOT what you want for
                 business data, and mentioning it as an option
                 for a tabular problem is a tell.

⭐ AND THE HONEST NOTE ON TABULAR DATA: for a table of numbers
  and categories, GRADIENT BOOSTING WINS. Not deep learning.
  XGBoost and its relatives beat neural networks on this shape
  of problem consistently, they train in seconds, and they tell
  you which features mattered. That is exactly why this project
  uses one.
ML 10

The workflow

① FRAME THE PROBLEM ⭐ the most skipped what decision will this change? who acts on it, and what do they do differently? what is the cost of a false positive versus a false negative? ⚠ "predict attrition" is not a problem statement. "Give a manager a ranked list of five people to talk to this month" is — and it dictates the metric, the threshold and the interface. ② GET AND UNDERSTAND THE DATA ← reflex 08 ③ CLEAN AND ENGINEER FEATURES ← reflexes 06, 07 ④ SPLIT ← reflex 11 ⭐ BEFORE any transformation is fitted. ⑤ ESTABLISH A BASELINE ⭐ non-negotiable the majority class · a simple rule · logistic regression. ⇒ a model that cannot beat "nobody leaves" is not a model, and you cannot know that without measuring it. ⑥ TRAIN A FEW CANDIDATES ⑦ EVALUATE ON VALIDATION ← reflex 16 ⑧ TUNE ← reflex 16 ⑨ TEST ONCE, ON THE HELD-OUT SET ⭐ ONCE. See reflex 11. ⑩ DEPLOY AND MONITOR ← reflex 17 ⭐ WHERE THE TIME ACTUALLY GOES: steps ② and ③ are most of the project. Step ⑥ is an afternoon. Beginners invert this, spend the time on algorithms, and get worse results. ⚠ AND THE STEP PEOPLE SKIP THAT COSTS THE MOST: ⑤. Without a baseline, "83% accurate" is meaningless — if 95% of employees stay, predicting "stays" for everyone scores 95% and is completely useless.
ML 11

Splits and validation

Three sets, three jobs, and one rule
TRAINING     ~60–70%  the model learns from this
VALIDATION   ~15–20%  you choose hyperparameters using this
TEST         ~15–20%  ⭐ TOUCHED ONCE, AT THE VERY END

⭐ WHY THE TEST SET MUST BE TOUCHED ONCE: every time you look at
  it and change something, you leak information about it into
  your choices. Do it ten times and your test score is an
  optimistic fiction — you have tuned to that specific sample,
  and production will disagree.
  ⇒ this is a discipline problem, not a technical one, and it is
    the most commonly broken rule in applied ML.

CROSS-VALIDATION — when data is limited
  split into k folds; train on k−1, validate on the held-out
  one; rotate; average the scores.
  ⇒ every row is used for both training and validation, and you
    get a SPREAD of scores rather than one. ⭐ THE SPREAD IS THE
    USEFUL PART: 0.81 ± 0.02 is a result; 0.81 ± 0.15 means your
    single number was luck.

⚠ THE THREE SPLITS THAT MUST NOT BE RANDOM

  TIME SERIES — ⭐ never random. Training on 2026 to predict
    2024 is predicting the past from the future. Split by DATE,
    always, and validate forward.
    ⇒ for attrition this matters: the split must be "train on
      people who left before date X, predict after".

  GROUPED DATA — if one employee has many rows, random
    splitting puts the same person in train and test. The model
    memorises the person and scores brilliantly on nothing.
    Split by GROUP.

  IMBALANCED CLASSES — with 5% leavers, a random split can give
    a test set with almost none. STRATIFY so the proportion is
    preserved in every set.
ML 12

Bias, variance and the trade

UNDERFITTING OVERFITTING (high bias) (high variance) too simple to capture the pattern memorised the training data, including its noise train score: low train score: excellent test score: low test score: poor ⭐ THE GAP IS THE DIAGNOSIS fix: more features, a more fix: more data, fewer flexible model, train longer features, regularisation, simpler model, early stopping ⭐ HOW TO READ IT IN PRACTICE both scores low → underfitting. The model is not capable enough. train high, test much lower → overfitting. Classic, and the most common state of a first attempt. both high and close → ✅ this is what you want. test HIGHER than train → ⚠ suspicious. Usually a leak, or an easier test split. REGULARISATION — penalising complexity on purpose add a cost for large parameters, so the model must justify every bit of complexity with a real reduction in error. ⇒ L1 can drive parameters to exactly zero (so it selects features); L2 shrinks them smoothly. ⭐ PARAMETERS vs HYPERPARAMETERS — the distinction that makes tuning make sense PARAMETERS learned FROM the data during training HYPERPARAMETERS chosen BY YOU before training — tree depth, learning rate, regularisation strength ⇒ you tune hyperparameters on the VALIDATION set. Tuning them by looking at the test set is exactly the sin in reflex 11.
ML 13 · the silent killer

Leakage

The failure with the best-looking symptoms

Leakage is information in your training data that will not be available at prediction time. It produces excellent validation scores and a model that fails in production — and because the symptom is success, nobody investigates.

The one habit that catches it: a suspiciously good result is a bug report, not a celebration. 0.99 AUC on a human-behaviour problem means you have leaked something, every time.

The five kinds, with HR examples
①  TARGET LEAKAGE — a feature that is a consequence of the
   outcome, not a cause available beforehand.
   ⭐ THE CLASSIC HERE: `exit_interview_completed`,
     `handover_document_uploaded`, `access_revoked_date`,
     `last_day`. Every one of them is a perfect predictor of
     leaving and is only known AFTER the decision.
   ⇒ THE TEST: would this value have existed, with this value,
     at the moment I need the prediction?

②  TRAIN–TEST CONTAMINATION — fitting a transformation on all
   the data before splitting. The scaler, the imputation median,
   the encoder, the feature-selection step. All of them must be
   fitted on training data only.
   ⇒ this is precisely why scikit-learn Pipelines exist
     (reflex 17): they make the correct order the default.

③  TEMPORAL LEAKAGE — a random split on time-ordered data, or a
   feature computed over a window that includes the future.
   "Team attrition rate" computed over the whole period leaks
   the outcome of the very person you are predicting.

④  GROUP LEAKAGE — the same employee in both train and test.

⑤  DUPLICATE LEAKAGE — the same row present twice, once in each
   set. Deduplicate BEFORE splitting.

⭐ THE PROCEDURE THAT PREVENTS ALL FIVE
  · split first, transform second, always
  · put every transformation inside a pipeline
  · for each feature, ask the "known at prediction time?"
    question explicitly
  · check feature importance: ⭐ IF ONE FEATURE DOMINATES
    ABSURDLY, IT IS PROBABLY LEAKAGE, not a discovery
  · and validate on a genuinely later time period before
    trusting anything
ML 14

The supervised algorithms

AlgorithmThe ideaWhen, and the catch
Linear regressionFit a straight line minimising squared errorA numeric target with roughly linear relationships. Fully interpretable. Catch: assumes linearity, and outliers dominate it.
Logistic regressionFit a line, then squash it to 0–1The baseline for classification, always. Fast, calibrated, explainable coefficients. Catch: only linear boundaries.
Decision treeA learned chain of if/else splitsWhen you must show the rule. Needs no scaling, handles mixed types. Catch: overfits badly alone — a deep tree memorises.
Random forestMany trees on random subsets, then vote — baggingStrong, forgiving default. Reduces variance. Catch: larger, slower, less interpretable than one tree.
Gradient boosting / XGBoostTrees built in sequence, each correcting the last's errors — boostingUsually the best on tabular data, and what this project uses. Reduces bias. Catch: more hyperparameters, and it will overfit without early stopping.
SVMFind the widest separating marginSmall datasets, many features, clear separation. Catch: needs scaling, slow on large data, no natural probability.
KNNLook at the k nearest examples and copy themA simple, intuitive baseline. Catch: needs scaling, all the cost is at prediction time, and it degrades badly in high dimensions.
Choosing, in practice
1  START WITH LOGISTIC REGRESSION (or linear, for a number).
   ⭐ IT IS YOUR BASELINE AND SOMETIMES YOUR ANSWER.
2  THEN TRY GRADIENT BOOSTING. On tabular data it is usually the
   winner, and the gap tells you whether the extra complexity
   buys anything.
3  ONLY GO FURTHER IF THE GAP JUSTIFIES IT.

⭐ BAGGING vs BOOSTING — one sentence each, and it is worth
  being able to say them:
    BAGGING (forest)   many independent models, averaged →
                       reduces VARIANCE
    BOOSTING (XGBoost) sequential models, each fixing the
                       previous one's mistakes → reduces BIAS

⚠ AND THE INTERPRETABILITY TRADE, WHICH IS NOT A TECHNICAL
  QUESTION HERE: a decision affecting someone's job needs an
  explanation a human can give and defend. A logistic regression
  offers coefficients. A boosted ensemble offers feature
  importances and per-prediction attributions — useful, but a
  step further from "here is the reason".
  ⇒ in this domain, a slightly less accurate model you can
    explain may be the correct engineering choice.
ML 15

Unsupervised learning

No labels — find structure
CLUSTERING — group similar rows
  K-MEANS       you choose k; it finds k centres. Fast, and it
                assumes round, similar-sized groups.
                ⚠ REQUIRES SCALING, and k is your choice — the
                  elbow method suggests, it does not decide.
  HIERARCHICAL  builds a tree of nested groups; you cut it where
                you like. No k up front. Slow on large data.
  DBSCAN        density-based. Finds arbitrary shapes and
                labels outliers as noise. ⭐ no k needed.

  ⚠ THE HONEST WARNING: CLUSTERING ALWAYS RETURNS CLUSTERS.
    Ask for four and you get four, whether or not the data has
    any group structure at all. ⭐ THE VALIDATION IS WHETHER THE
    GROUPS MEAN SOMETHING TO A DOMAIN EXPERT — there is no
    accuracy score to hide behind.

DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION
  PCA        find the directions of greatest variance; keep the
             first few. ⭐ USEFUL FOR compression, collinearity
             and visualisation. ⚠ the new axes are combinations,
             so interpretability is gone.
  t-SNE/UMAP for VISUALISATION ONLY. ⚠ distances and cluster
             sizes in the picture are not meaningful, and people
             over-read these plots constantly.

ANOMALY DETECTION — find the unusual
  isolation forest, one-class SVM, or simply distance from a
  cluster centre.
  ⇒ in HR: unusual access patterns, expense outliers, data-entry
    errors. ⚠ and remember the base rate — anomalies are rare by
    definition, so precision will be low.

⭐ WHERE THIS IS ACTUALLY USEFUL IN THIS PROJECT: employee
  SEGMENTATION for tailored retention approaches, and finding
  data-quality problems. Not for predicting attrition — that has
  labels, so it is a supervised problem and using clustering for
  it throws the labels away.
ML 16 · read this twice

Evaluation

THE CONFUSION MATRIX — everything comes from these four cells PREDICTED leave stay ACTUAL leave TP 45 FN 5 ← missed leavers stay FP 95 TN 855 ← false alarms ACCURACY = (TP+TN)/all = 90% ⭐ AND IT IS ALMOST USELESS HERE. Predict "stay" for everyone and you score 95%. ⚠ NEVER USE ACCURACY ON IMBALANCED DATA — it is the single most misleading number in applied ML. PRECISION = TP/(TP+FP) = 45/140 = 32% "when we flag someone, how often are we right?" ⇒ the cost of being wrong is a manager's wasted time and an awkward conversation. RECALL = TP/(TP+FN) = 45/50 = 90% "of everyone who left, how many did we catch?" ⇒ the cost of being wrong is losing someone you could have retained. ⭐ THE TRADE IS UNAVOIDABLE AND IT IS A BUSINESS DECISION, NOT A TECHNICAL ONE. Lower the threshold: recall rises, precision falls. Raise it: the reverse. ⇒ SO ASK WHICH ERROR COSTS MORE, and set the threshold from the answer — never leave it at the default 0.5 and call it neutral. F1 = the harmonic mean of precision and recall one number when you need one. ⚠ it hides which side you are failing on, so never report it alone. ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC ROC-AUC ranking quality across all thresholds. ⚠ looks flatteringly high on imbalanced data because true negatives are abundant. PR-AUC ⭐ THE RIGHT ONE FOR A RARE POSITIVE CLASS. CALIBRATION — the check nobody runs if the model says 0.7, do 70% of those people actually leave? ⇒ if a number will be shown to a human as a probability, it must be calibrated, or the number is misleading even when the ranking is good. FOR REGRESSION MAE average absolute error, in real units. Interpretable. RMSE punishes large errors more. Same units. R² proportion of variance explained. ⚠ easily inflated by adding features, and meaningless out of context. ⭐ AND ALWAYS, ALWAYS COMPARE TO THE BASELINE. A metric without a baseline is a number without a meaning.
ML 17

Pipeline and deployment

From notebook to the service on port 8000
THE PIPELINE OBJECT — one artefact, correct by construction
  Pipeline([ imputer, encoder, scaler, model ])
  ⭐ WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN CONVENIENCE: the pipeline is
    FITTED AS ONE THING on training data and APPLIED AS ONE
    THING at prediction. That makes contamination (reflex 13)
    structurally difficult rather than merely discouraged.
  ⇒ and it guarantees the same preprocessing in training and in
    production, which is otherwise the classic source of
    "it scored well and predicts nonsense".

SERIALISATION
  save the whole pipeline, not just the model.
  ⚠ AND SAVE ITS CONTEXT WITH IT: the library versions, the
    feature names and their ORDER, the training date, the
    metrics it achieved. A model file alone is not reproducible
    — a different library version can load it and predict
    differently.

SERVING — what this project does
  FastAPI loads the pipeline ONCE at startup (loading is slow,
  the object is reusable), then each request validates its input
  with Pydantic, builds the features, predicts, and returns a
  score with a level.
  ⚠ and per Volume III, reflex 15: inference is CPU-bound, so it
    belongs in a plain `def`, not an `async def`.

VERSIONING
  the model has a version, and every prediction records which
  version produced it. ⭐ WITHOUT THIS YOU CANNOT ANSWER "WHY
  DID THIS SCORE CHANGE?" — and someone will ask.

MONITORING — ⭐ THE PART EVERYONE SKIPS
  ① IS IT RUNNING? latency, error rate, throughput.
  ② IS THE INPUT STILL THE SAME SHAPE? ⭐ DATA DRIFT: the
    distribution of incoming features moves away from training.
    A reorganisation changes every department code and the model
    silently degrades.
  ③ IS IT STILL RIGHT? ⚠ CONCEPT DRIFT: the relationship
    itself changes. And the honest problem — you only learn the
    true outcome MONTHS later, so accuracy is always measured in
    arrears.
  ④ IS ANYONE ACTING ON IT? a model nobody uses is a cost, not
    a feature.

⭐ AND THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST IN THIS DOMAIN: A RETENTION
  MODEL CHANGES BEHAVIOUR. Managers act on the flags, so the
  future data is shaped by the model's own predictions. Your
  next training set is no longer a clean observation of reality
  — plan for that, or your evaluation slowly becomes fiction.

DL 18

Neural networks

ONE NEURON take several inputs, multiply each by a WEIGHT, add them up, add a BIAS, then pass the result through a non-linear ACTIVATION function. inputs ──►(w₁·x₁ + w₂·x₂ + … + b)──► activation ──► output ⭐ WITHOUT THE ACTIVATION, STACKING LAYERS IS POINTLESS: a chain of linear operations is just one bigger linear operation. THE NON-LINEARITY IS WHAT MAKES DEPTH WORTH ANYTHING. A NETWORK — neurons in layers input layer ──► hidden layers ──► output layer features learned prediction representations ⭐ WHAT "LEARNED REPRESENTATIONS" MEANS, AND WHY IT IS THE WHOLE POINT: early layers learn simple patterns and later layers combine them into complex ones. For images: edges → textures → shapes → faces. Nobody specified edges. ⇒ this is why deep learning wins on images, audio and text — the useful features are hierarchical and too numerous to hand-engineer. ⇒ AND WHY IT LOSES ON TABLES: `tenure_years` is already a meaningful feature. There is no hierarchy to discover, so the advantage evaporates and gradient boosting wins.
DL 19

How they learn

The training loop, and the words attached to each step
repeat many times:
  ①  FORWARD PASS      push a batch through; get predictions
  ②  LOSS              measure how wrong they are
  ③  BACKPROPAGATION   ⭐ work out how much each weight
                       contributed to that error — the chain
                       rule, applied backwards through the
                       network
  ④  GRADIENT DESCENT  nudge every weight downhill by the
                       learning rate
  ⑤  next batch

VOCABULARY YOU WILL SEE IN EVERY TUTORIAL
  EPOCH          one full pass over the training data
  BATCH SIZE     how many examples per weight update
  LEARNING RATE  ⭐ the step size, and the first thing to tune.
                 Too high diverges; too low never arrives.
  OPTIMISER      the strategy for stepping. Adam is the default
                 and a reasonable one.
  DROPOUT        randomly ignore some neurons while training, so
                 the network cannot rely on any single path —
                 regularisation for networks.
  EARLY STOPPING stop when validation error stops improving.
                 ⭐ THE SIMPLEST AND MOST EFFECTIVE ANTI-
                   OVERFITTING TOOL, and it applies to XGBoost
                   too.

WHY GPUs
  the core operation is matrix multiplication, thousands of
  identical small calculations at once. A CPU has a few fast
  cores; a GPU has thousands of slower ones. ⇒ 10–100× for this
  specific shape of work, which is why hardware became a topic.

⚠ AND THE PRACTICAL REALITY: deep learning needs a lot of data,
  a lot of compute, and a lot of tuning. With 5,000 employee
  records it will lose to gradient boosting, take longer, and
  explain itself less. That is not a criticism of the technique
  — it is the wrong tool for this shape of problem.

DL 21

Natural language processing

Why language is hard, and the three generations of answer
WHY IT IS HARD
  ambiguity ("bank") · context over long distances · sarcasm and
  negation · word order carrying meaning · spelling, slang,
  multiple languages · and ⭐ THE SAME MEANING IN COMPLETELY
  DIFFERENT WORDS, which is what breaks keyword approaches.

TURNING TEXT INTO NUMBERS — the actual problem
  ① BAG OF WORDS / TF-IDF
     count words; weight down the ones that appear everywhere.
     ⇒ still a strong, fast, interpretable baseline for
       classification. ⚠ word order and meaning are discarded
       entirely.
  ② WORD EMBEDDINGS
     each word becomes a vector learned from the contexts it
     appears in, so similar words land near each other.
     ⭐ MEANING BECAME GEOMETRY. ⚠ one vector per word, so
       "bank" has a single blended meaning.
  ③ CONTEXTUAL EMBEDDINGS (transformers)
     ⭐ the vector depends on the sentence. "River bank" and
       "bank account" now differ. This is the unlock behind
       modern NLP.

CLASSIC TASKS
  sentiment · named-entity recognition · classification ·
  summarisation · translation · question answering

PREPROCESSING — and the caveat that matters now
  tokenise · lowercase · remove stop words · stem or lemmatise
  ⚠ MOST OF THIS IS OBSOLETE FOR TRANSFORMER MODELS AND CAN HURT
    THEM. Removing "not" destroys the meaning you are trying to
    classify. ⇒ heavy preprocessing belongs with TF-IDF; modern
    models want the text roughly as written.

⭐ WHERE IT LANDS IN THIS PROJECT: free-text exit-interview and
  survey comments are a natural fit for a model, and everything
  in Part V applies. ⚠ AND THEY ARE THE MOST SENSITIVE DATA IN
  THE BUILDING — see reflex 25.

AI 22

What an LLM is

One mechanism, and everything follows from it
A transformer trained on an enormous amount of text to do exactly
one thing: PREDICT THE NEXT TOKEN. Then repeat, feeding each
prediction back in.

  "The capital of France is" → { " Paris": 0.94, … }

⭐ THAT IS THE WHOLE MECHANISM. Everything it appears to do —
  reasoning, summarising, writing code — is this, repeated.

THE THREE TRAINING STAGES, AND WHAT EACH EXPLAINS
  PRETRAINING          next-token prediction over a huge corpus.
                       ⇒ where the knowledge comes from, AND the
                         KNOWLEDGE CUTOFF — the corpus stops at
                         a date and the model does not know it.
  INSTRUCTION TUNING   examples of instruction → good answer.
                       ⇒ why it answers rather than continuing
                         your text.
  PREFERENCE TUNING    humans compare pairs; the model is tuned
                       toward the preferred one.
                       ⇒ why it has that tone, that hedging, and
                         those refusals.

FOUR CONSEQUENCES YOU MUST DESIGN AROUND
  ① TOKENS, NOT CHARACTERS. It cannot reliably count letters or
    reverse a string — it does not see them.
  ② ⭐ IT HALLUCINATES STRUCTURALLY. It produces PLAUSIBLE
    continuations and has no truth flag and no reliable "I don't
    know" signal. An invented citation and a real one are
    produced identically. ⇒ you cannot prompt this away; you
    engineer around it.
  ③ NOT DETERMINISTIC. The same prompt can give a different
    answer, so nothing downstream may assume an exact string and
    you cannot test with equality.
  ④ THE API IS STATELESS. A "conversation" is your code
    resending the whole history every turn — and paying for all
    of it, every turn.

⭐ THE ARCHITECTURAL RULE THIS PROJECT FOLLOWS:
  THE DETERMINISTIC SYSTEM COMPUTES; THE MODEL NARRATES.
  XGBoost produces the risk score — reproducible and auditable.
  The LLM writes the explanation around it. So the worst
  possible hallucination is a clumsy sentence, never a wrong
  number in an HR review.
AI 23

Prompting

In order of how much it actually helps
1  THE RIGHT CONTEXT        ⭐ by far the biggest lever. Most bad
                            output is MISSING INFORMATION, not
                            bad wording.
2  A CONSTRAINED OUTPUT     a schema or an enum cannot be
                            hallucinated into. ⭐ This REMOVES a
                            failure mode rather than reducing it.
3  EXAMPLES FOR EDGE CASES  3–5 input/output pairs beat any
                            adjective. Showing a 40-word answer
                            works; asking for "concise" does not.
4  A CLEAR ROLE AND TASK    who it is, what it is doing, what
                            it must not do.
5  REWORDING                ⚠ the smallest lever, and the one
                            people spend all their time on.

⚠ OBSOLETE ADVICE: "think step by step". Current models reason
  by default, and telling them not to actively degrades output.

⭐ THE DEBUGGING RULE: WHEN OUTPUT IS BAD, READ THE FULLY
  RENDERED PROMPT — the actual bytes sent, every variable
  interpolated. A surprising share of prompt bugs are visible
  immediately: an empty variable, a truncated document,
  retrieved context that is entirely irrelevant. Then ask:
  could a competent human do this task with only this context?
  If not, no rewording will fix an absence.

AND THE SECURITY ONE, WHICH IS NOT OPTIONAL HERE
  ⭐ PROMPT INJECTION: anything that goes into the context can
    influence the output. A CV, a survey comment or an
    uploaded document is written by someone else, and that
    person is now speaking to your model.
  ⇒ THE ONLY REAL DEFENCE IS THE TRUST BOUNDARY: whatever the
    model asks to do runs with the PERMISSIONS OF THE PERSON IN
    THE SESSION, enforced in code (Volume II, reflex 09). Then
    an injection that fully succeeds still cannot do anything
    that user could not already do.
  ⚠ "ignore instructions in the document" is a mitigation, never
    the control.

COST AND SPEED, BRIEFLY
  output tokens cost several times input tokens and dominate
  latency ⇒ "answer in one sentence" is a real performance
  control. And caching a stable prompt prefix is the first cost
  lever, before changing anything else.
AI 24

Embeddings and RAG

AN EMBEDDING — meaning as geometry text → a vector of floats, where SEMANTIC closeness becomes GEOMETRIC closeness. "cancel my order" ─┐ close, with NO words "how do I get a refund" ─┘ in common ⭐ ⇒ that is exactly what keyword search cannot do, and it is the whole value. ⚠ AND THE THREE PREDICTABLE FAILURES NEGATION "invoices NOT paid" retrieves paid invoices — a sentence and its negation embed close EXACT IDS product codes, employee numbers, error codes are semantically meaningless ⇒ ⭐ THIS IS WHY HYBRID (keyword + vector) SEARCH EXISTS NUMBERS "over £500 in March" is a FILTER, not a similarity question — use metadata ⚠ and changing the embedding model means RE-EMBEDDING EVERYTHING. Vectors from two models are not comparable. RAG — retrieval-augmented generation the model does not know your documents and its knowledge stops at a date. So fetch the relevant text at query time and put it in the prompt. documents ─chunk─► embed ─► vector store │ question ──embed──────────────────┤ ▼ retrieve top-k │ rerank │ context + question ──► LLM │ answer + sources ⭐ THE MOST USEFUL THING TO KNOW: WHEN THE ANSWER IS WRONG, THERE ARE FOUR POSSIBLE CAUSES AND YOU MUST SEPARATE THEM ① was it in the corpus at all? (coverage) ② was it in the retrieved set? (recall) ③ did it survive reranking? (precision) ④ did the model use it? (faithfulness) without this split, debugging RAG is guesswork — and teams spend weeks tuning chunk size when the document was never ingested. THE CHEAPEST QUALITY WIN prepend the document title and section heading to every chunk. "…must be approved by HR" is useless without knowing which policy it belongs to. ⚠ AND CHECK WHETHER YOU NEED IT AT ALL: context windows are now very large. If the whole corpus fits in a cached prompt, you get no vector store, no chunking and no retrieval bugs. RAG earns its complexity when the corpus far exceeds the window, changes constantly, or needs per-user access filtering — ⭐ WHICH HR DOCUMENTS DO. RAG vs FINE-TUNING — the distinction people get backwards RAG adds KNOWLEDGE. Citable, updatable, filterable by permission. FINE-TUNING adds STYLE, FORMAT and CONSISTENCY. ⚠ FINE-TUNING IS NOT HOW YOU ADD FACTS. Facts baked into weights are uncitable, stale on change, and unverifiable.
AI 25

Agents and tool use

The loop, and the test for whether you need one
TOOL USE — the mechanism
  you describe functions the model may call. It does not execute
  them: it REQUESTS a call, YOUR CODE runs it, and you return
  the result.
  ⭐ THE MODEL NEVER RUNS ANYTHING. It asks. Your code decides
    whether to comply — and that gap is where all your security
    lives.

THE ESCALATION, AND WHERE TO STOP
  ONE CALL         you write the prompt. Deterministic, cheap,
                   easy to evaluate.
  CHAINED CALLS    you write the sequence. Each step separately
                   evaluable. ⭐ MOST PRODUCTION VALUE IS HERE,
                   and it is deeply unglamorous.
  ROUTED           a classifier picks a branch. Still bounded.
  AGENT            THE MODEL chooses the sequence. Unbounded
                   paths, unbounded cost, every run different.

⭐ THE TEST: CAN YOU ENUMERATE THE STEPS IN ADVANCE?
  yes → build a pipeline and get determinism, per-step
        evaluation and lower cost for free.
  genuinely no → an agent, with caps.
⚠ AGENTS ARE THE MOST OVER-APPLIED PATTERN IN THE FIELD. Most
  tasks people build agents for can be enumerated — which makes
  them pipelines wearing a costume.

WHAT MAKES ONE OPERABLE (non-negotiable)
  · an ITERATION CAP — an unbounded loop is an unbounded bill
  · a TOKEN BUDGET
  · a FULL TRACE of every call, argument and result
  · a CONTEXT STRATEGY — tool results accumulate forever
  · ⭐ TOOLS SCOPED TO THE CALLER, built from the verified
    session — not from anything the model said

AND THE EVALUATION POINT THAT UNDERPINS ALL OF PART V
  ⭐ A SET OF GRADED EXAMPLES, RUN ON EVERY CHANGE, IS THE
    DIFFERENCE BETWEEN ENGINEERING AND VIBES. Twenty cases in a
    file beats any impression. ⚠ WITHOUT IT NOBODY WILL DARE
    TOUCH THE PROMPT — which is how a nine-month-old prompt with
    a superstition in it ends up frozen in production.

Analytics 27

KPIs and metrics

Designing a number people will act on
A GOOD METRIC IS
  · DEFINED precisely enough that two people compute the same
    value — ⭐ INCLUDING the numerator, the denominator, the time
    window, the timezone, and who is excluded
  · ACTIONABLE — somebody can change it
  · COMPARABLE over time, which means the definition is frozen

⚠ LEADING vs LAGGING
  attrition RATE is lagging — by the time it moves, the people
  have gone. Survey scores, internal-transfer requests and
  manager-change events are leading. ⇒ a dashboard of only
  lagging metrics reports history and cannot prompt action.

⭐ GOODHART'S LAW — the one to design against
  "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good
  measure." Target attrition rate and you will get reclassified
  departures, delayed terminations, and pressure not to record
  regretted leavers as regretted.
  ⇒ so pair every target with a GUARDRAIL metric that catches
    the obvious gaming, and expect the gaming rather than being
    surprised by it.

VANITY METRICS — big, flattering, and useless
  "1.2 million rows processed." Nobody decides anything with it.
  ⭐ THE TEST: IF THIS NUMBER DOUBLED, WHAT WOULD WE DO
    DIFFERENTLY? No answer ⇒ do not put it on the dashboard.

HR METRICS THAT ACTUALLY GET USED
  headcount and its movement · attrition, split REGRETTED vs
  not ⭐ (the split is the whole point — voluntary departures of
  people you wanted to keep is a different number from total
  turnover) · time to hire · offer acceptance · internal
  mobility · absence · engagement · cost per hire · tenure
  distribution — not average tenure

⚠ AND THE PRIVACY FLOOR, WHICH IS A HARD RULE IN THIS DOMAIN:
  ⭐ ENFORCE A MINIMUM CELL SIZE. "Engagement in the Lisbon
    team" where the team is three people identifies three
    people. Aggregation is not anonymisation, and a filter
    combination is a re-identification tool.
Analytics 28

Dashboards and visualisation

Four artefacts people constantly confuse
DASHBOARD  monitored repeatedly. Few numbers, always the same
           ones, answering "is anything wrong?"
REPORT     a periodic snapshot with commentary.
ANALYSIS   a one-off answer to one question. ⭐ MOST REQUESTS
           FOR A DASHBOARD ARE ACTUALLY THIS — and building the
           dashboard means maintaining it forever to answer a
           question asked once.
ALERT      tells you when something crosses a line. ⇒ if the
           purpose is "notice when attrition spikes", an alert
           is correct and a dashboard is a worse version of it.

INFORMATION DESIGN — the three time horizons
  5 SECONDS  is anything wrong? ⇒ the top row
  30 SECONDS where and how much? ⇒ the middle
  5 MINUTES  why? ⇒ drill-downs below

CHART CHOICE IS NOT TASTE
  ⭐ the accuracy of a visual encoding is measurable, and it
    ranks: POSITION > LENGTH > ANGLE > AREA > COLOUR > SHAPE.
    ⇒ which is why a bar chart beats a pie for comparison,
      every time.
  compare categories → bar        over time → line
  distribution       → histogram  relationship → scatter
  part of a whole    → stacked bar, or just a table

⚠ WHAT TO AVOID, AND WHY
  · pie charts beyond three slices — angles are hard to compare
  · ⭐ TRUNCATED Y-AXES on bar charts — the bar's LENGTH is the
    encoding, so cutting the axis lies about the ratio
  · dual axes — any correlation can be manufactured by scaling
  · 3D anything, and rainbow palettes on ordered data

⭐ AND THE MOST UNDER-USED TOOL: ANNOTATION. A line on the chart
  saying "reorganisation" where the step change is explains more
  than any amount of styling — and it travels with the
  screenshot, which is how the chart will actually be seen.

WHAT NOT TO PUT ON A DASHBOARD
  anything nobody acts on · anything that needs a paragraph to
  interpret · a number whose definition is disputed ⭐ (fix the
  definition first, or the dashboard becomes the argument)

Term bank

Volume IV · the vocabulary of the AI service
feature
An input column the model reads. Engineering them usually beats changing algorithm.
target
The thing being predicted. Here: did this person leave.
base rate
How often the target actually occurs. Governs metric choice and every interpretation.
MNAR
Missing for a reason related to the missing value itself — the case where imputation biases.
leakage
Training information unavailable at prediction time. Great scores, useless model.
overfitting
Memorising the training data. Diagnosed by the train–test gap.
regularisation
Penalising complexity so the model must earn it.
hyperparameter
Set by you before training; tuned on validation, never on test.
stratify
Preserve class proportions when splitting imbalanced data.
bagging
Many independent models averaged — cuts variance. Random forest.
boosting
Sequential models each fixing the last's errors — cuts bias. XGBoost.
precision
Of those flagged, how many were right.
recall
Of those who mattered, how many were caught.
PR-AUC
The ranking metric to use when the positive class is rare.
calibration
Whether a predicted 0.7 really means 70%. Required if a human reads the number.
pipeline
Preprocessing plus model as one fitted artefact — the structural defence against leakage.
data drift
Incoming feature distributions moving away from training data.
concept drift
The underlying relationship itself changing.
token
The unit an LLM reads and writes — roughly ¾ of a word, not a character.
hallucination
A confident, plausible, false output. Structural, not a bug.
embedding
A vector where semantic closeness is geometric closeness.
RAG
Retrieving relevant text at query time and putting it in the prompt.
prompt injection
Instructions hidden in content the model reads. Defended by a trust boundary in code.
eval
A graded example set run on every change. The difference between engineering and vibes.
Goodhart's law
A measure made into a target stops being a good measure.
minimum cell size
The privacy floor below which an aggregate identifies individuals.
